# deberta-v3-xsmall-pii — v2.1

BIO token classifier for personally identifiable information (PII) in English text. Same 8 labels
and same training configuration as v2. v2.1 targets **ADDRESS**: residential and office addresses
written on one line or across several lines, in the formats of the firm's jurisdictions
(Singapore, India, UAE, UK, Hong Kong, ASEAN, wider Asia).

## Labels

Unchanged from v2: `PERSON`, `BUSINESS`, `ADDRESS`, `DOB`, `ACCOUNT`, `PHONE`, `EMAIL`, `TIN`
(BIO, 17 labels). **ADDRESS definition tightened:** full residential / office addresses and their
parts (street, building, unit, city, postcode, state, country) when they form someone's address.
A bare place name in running text ("expanding into France", "Thai cuisine") is no longer ADDRESS.

## What changed from v2

1. **The model can see line breaks and tabs.** DeBERTa-v3's SentencePiece tokenizer turns `\n` and
   `\t` into plain spaces, so v1/v2 could not tell `456 Ocean Way⏎Liverpool` from one line, or where a
   form field ended. They are now shown as in-vocabulary marker tokens (`▁¶` for a line break,
   `▁|` for a tab) with character offsets mapped back to the original text
   (`tokenize_bio.visible_breaks`). Marker tokens carry no label and do not end a span, so a
   multi-line address is one `B-I-I…` entity. The setting is stored in the model config
   (`visible_breaks`) and read by `PIIPredictor`, so v1/v2 weights keep their original tokenization.
2. **Bare place names → `O`.** An ADDRESS span made only of city / state / county / country parts,
   with no address-field cue before it on the same line ("City:", "Address:", "Country of
   residence"), becomes `O` and is recorded as a `place` look-alike for evaluation
   (Nemotron 11,299 and AI4Privacy 4,598 spans in train).
3. **Gretel street-address tails masked.** Gretel labels only the street part of
   `0567 Drake Road, Manchester, M12 4BE, UK`; the unlabelled continuation up to the end of the line
   is now `IGNORE` (2,072 in train) so a complete prediction is not trained as wrong.
4. **Real public addresses in context.** 20,848 real addresses (below) placed in 8,000 training
   records: e-mail sign-offs (name / job title / company / address / phone), attention and delivery
   blocks ("Attn:", "Bill To:", often upper case), letters, KYC / change-of-particulars forms
   (residential + employer office address), statement headers (bank address + client address) and
   one-line sentences. Half single-line, half multi-line; a third get an office unit / level prefix
   (`Level 12`, `#04-56`, `Suite 1203`, `12/F`).

## Address data (real, public)

`src/address_sources.py` → `data/addresses/addresses.jsonl` (90% train / 10% stress, split by hash).

| Source | License | Use | Addresses |
|---|---|---|---|
| [ellenhp/libpostal](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ellenhp/libpostal) `openstreetmap_addresses` (12 of 71 shards) | ODbL (OpenStreetMap) | street-level addresses rebuilt from libpostal component tags into lines; Latin script only | 17,350 |
| [ellenhp/libpostal](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ellenhp/libpostal) `uk_openaddresses` | OpenAddresses UK | UK house number / street / town / postcode | 1,499 |
| [gagan1985/indian-addresses-raw](https://huggingface.co/datasets/gagan1985/indian-addresses-raw) | Apache-2.0 | Indian registered offices (MCA company registry) and bank branches, as written | 1,999 |

Per country: India 3,499 · UK 3,000 · Singapore 2,990 · Hong Kong 2,500 · Indonesia 1,500 ·
Malaysia 1,500 · Philippines 1,000 · UAE 820 · Kuwait 600 · Saudi Arabia 600 · Thailand 560 ·
Bangladesh 500 · Sri Lanka 500 · Pakistan 500 · China 355 · Qatar 146 · Vietnam 125 · Oman 54 ·
Korea 35 · Taiwan 27 · Japan 21 · Bahrain 16. (UAE, China and East Asia are limited by how few
Latin-script, street-level addresses OpenStreetMap has for them.)

Filters: a road plus a house number / postcode / PO box / unit, or a named building on a road in a
city ("venue, city" entries are dropped); duplicates removed; Indian rows de-duplicated part by
part and limited to 3–8 parts with a 6-digit PIN.

Why a sample and not the whole worldwide set: the records must teach *where an address starts and
stops inside a document*; hundreds of millions of bare address strings would swamp the 66k
documents, push the model to over-tag place-like text and are dominated by Europe / the US.

## Development notes

- **First v2.1 run discarded (label bug).** The first training run used marker tokens that were
  labelled `O` and *ended* the current span, so every multi-line address was taught as one address
  per line. It found 98.6% of stress addresses but only 66.7% with exact boundaries (precision
  0.275). Fixed in `tokenize_bio.token_labels` (markers are unlabelled and keep the span open), with
  a regression test (`test_multiline_span_stays_one_entity`).
- **Second run: multi-line over-extension.** After the fix, single-line addresses were 91–99%
  exact, but multi-line addresses in e-mail signatures (3.8%) and envelopes (15%) absorbed the
  neighbouring name / job-title / company lines: every multi-line block seen in training contained
  only address lines. The final training set adds sign-off and attention-block records where an
  address sits next to other lines (different wording and job titles from the stress set). The
  stress set is generated from its own seed and was unchanged by this.

Look-alikes tagged as PII (stress): amounts 1.0% (v2 0.9%), codes 4.6% (v2 4.1%; mostly codes

**How to read this.** The stress layouts (e-mail signature with "Relationship Manager", "TO:"
envelope, CSV row, verification sentence) and the stress addresses never appear in training, but
the final training set contains *similar* sign-off and attention-block layouts with different
wording, so part of the multi-line gain reflects layout familiarity. Remaining weak spots:
multi-line exact boundaries (58.9%; boundaries still drift by a line in signatures/envelopes),
Indian addresses (59.5% exact — long, irregular bank-branch strings), codes read as ADDRESS.

## Limitations

- **Regression vs v2 on bare `A/C` cues.** In very short inputs, a hyphenated account number after
  `A/C` (`A/C 0123-456789-0`) is missed (v2 found it); in a multi-line block it can be clipped
  (`-456789-0`). `Account No: …`, `account 0123-…` in sentences and form fields are detected. Likely
  cause: the new address records dilute v2's short `A/C` pattern; fix = more short `A/C` lines.
- Still only synthetic documents plus real addresses placed in templates; not yet measured on
  real documents.
- UAE (820), China (355) and East-Asian addresses are under-represented in the public data.
- Asian IDs (PAN, MyKad, NPWP) and Asian company-name forms (Sdn Bhd, Pte Ltd, PT) remain rare.
- English only.
